## Performance sample and limits

One 11-second local Chromium sample per width and implementation measured 660 frame intervals during the How replay. Original mock and application both measured p95 16.7 ms, maximum 16.8 ms, no intervals above 50 ms and no observed long tasks at 1280 and 390. A second comparison sampled Ticket photo travel, unfolding, folding and return: 163–165 frame intervals per implementation/width, p95 16.7 ms/max 16.8 ms, no >50 ms gaps or long tasks in either implementation. No page errors occurred in either comparison. The Ticket artifact is `.agent-context/motion/ticket-frame-comparison.json`. The raw How artifact is `.agent-context/motion/frame-comparison.json`. This is a local emulated-browser comparison on one machine, not a production benchmark or physical-phone frame-rate guarantee.

The explicit narrowing/reveal timings are part of matching the approved interaction. They do not change Search network/model time. Reduced motion removes the holds and animated flights. No production hosting, DNS, environment setting, merge or deployment is part of this follow-up. Physical-phone, screen-reader and real hosted Google/Spotify acceptance remain separate from Chromium and local Supabase checks.


Compressed resources referenced by the final prerendered home HTML (deduplicated script/link paths, Python gzip) are 337,575 B JavaScript, 53,296 B fonts and 12,745 B CSS: 403,616 B total. Restoring motion adds 15,467 B versus the incomplete November 7 redesign (388,149 B), while the total remains 102,654 B below the retained accounts baseline (506,270 B). These numbers exclude source photos, Turnstile and later dynamic SDK chunks, as in the earlier comparison. Native timelines avoid shipping GSAP in addition to the existing Motion package.
